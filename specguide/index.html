What is this spec about?

The 30-second version

We make online slot games. We're adding a "wall of fame" inside the games: a leaderboard showing the biggest wins other players got today and this week. See a big win you're curious about? Tap it and watch that exact round play out again, spin by spin, like a sports replay. The spec you're about to read is the instruction manual for building it: what the screens look like, what happens when you tap each thing, and who builds which part.

Leaderboard

Like an arcade high-score screen. Daily / weekly × winnings / multiplier: four boards, top 20 each.

Big win replay

Tap ▶ to replay the round in a new tab. The player presses SPIN. No money moves.

Go to that game

Tap the game image or name, confirm, and jump straight into that game.
Why we're making itPlayers see big wins really happen. A win in another game makes them curious to try it, one tap away. The replay lets them feel the moment instead of reading a number.
What it's notThe replay moves no money. It's a recording, like rewatching a goal. "REPLAYING" stays on screen so nobody thinks they won.

The spec answers four questions

RulesWhose wins count, how they rank, when boards reset
ScreensWhat every screen looks like, in every state
Who does whatArt, front end, back end and the game team
WordingEvery word on screen, in 14 languages
In one sentence

The spec is the blueprint for a "top wins of the day" board in our games, with an instant replay button, so everyone building it makes the same thing.

05 / 06 · What is a spec?

What is a spec?

In short

A spec (short for specification) is the shared instruction manual for building a feature. Before anything is drawn or coded, it writes down what the feature is for, how it should behave, what every screen looks like and who builds which part. Everyone on the project reads the same document, so everyone builds the same thing.

States the goal

Why the feature exists, who it's for, and what this version will and won't do.

Shows the screens

Mockups of every screen and every state, so nobody has to guess what it looks like.

Sets the rules

Exactly how the feature behaves, including the unusual cases and what happens when things go wrong.
Why we write oneWithout a spec, every team fills the gaps with its own guess. Writing it down settles questions once, before work starts, so there's less rework and fewer "that's not what I meant" moments.
What it's notIt isn't the code, the final artwork or a technical design. It says what the feature should do and look like; each team decides how to build its part.

A spec usually contains

Goal and scopeWhat we're building, and what we're not
RulesHow the feature behaves, case by case
User flowWhat the player does, step by step
Mockups and statesEvery screen, in every state
In one sentence

A spec is the one place where everyone agrees on what we're building, before we build it.

User flowStep by step, from how players enter to how they leave.榜單畫面(UI) 01
MockupA sketch or screenshot of every screen, so others can see what you want them to build. Number each part and explain it in a table: #, element, portrait / landscape, state, notes.榜單畫面(UI) 02
States and errorsLoading, empty, failed to load, fewer items than expected, disabled. What players see and can do in each.榜單畫面(UI) 03, 回放 07

Writing tips

  1. Portrait and landscapeThe same screen is laid out differently in each orientation: what stays fixed, what scrolls and where each element moves. Describe both, and check every screen in both.
  2. Will people understand it?Use plain words and short sentences, one idea per line. Call each screen and element by the same name everywhere. A labelled screenshot usually beats a paragraph. Have someone outside the project read it.
  3. Every state, not just the normal oneLoading, empty, failed to load, fewer items than expected, disabled buttons. If a state can happen, the spec should show it.
  4. Long text and other languagesTranslations and big amounts take more room than English. Leave space, or say what happens when text doesn't fit.
